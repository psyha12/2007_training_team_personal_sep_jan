### Personal Reflections 💭

I felt this was a really positive end to the project. From my perspective, we worked very well as a team and everyone contributed meaningfully throughout. I also felt that I learnt a lot from the other members of the group, particularly through reviewing code, discussing different approaches, and working through problems together.

One of the main reflections we discussed as a group was that it would have been useful to spend more time at the beginning agreeing our Git workflow. We could have been clearer around protecting the `main` branch, using a consistent branch-naming convention, and requiring a minimum number of reviewers before pull requests were merged. Although we adapted these practices as the project progressed, setting them out earlier would likely have reduced some of the merge conflicts we ran into later on.

I also think the project highlighted to me how valuable the planning and review stages are. Towards the end, much of my contribution involved reviewing requirements, resolving conflicts, updating documentation, and making sure the final repository was clear and usable. These tasks were less visible than building individual dashboard components, but they were important for bringing the project together into a finished output.

Looking back across the project, I am particularly pleased with the progress I made in R Shiny. At the start, I was much less confident with the structure of a Shiny application and expected to contribute mainly through documentation and analysis. By the end, I had a much clearer understanding of how the UI, server logic, reusable functions, and `app.R` fitted together, and I felt confident enough to contribute directly to the dashboard development.

Overall, I think the project was a useful experience in collaborative working, Git-based development, code review, documentation, and adapting to a less familiar technology. It also highlighted some clear improvements I would make in the next group project, particularly around agreeing development standards and repository rules earlier on in the process.
