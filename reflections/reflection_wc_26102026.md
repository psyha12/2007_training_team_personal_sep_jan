## Week 6 (w/c 26.10.26) Reflection

### What was discussed / covered (1)? 🗣️

- Reviewed the current state of the project and agreed a final plan for completing the remaining work.
- Discussed some Git and branch-management issues, including use of `.gitignore`, stashing changes, pull requests, and resolving merge conflicts.

- Confirmed ownership of the remaining dashboard sections so that everyone had a clear area to complete before final integration.

### Personal Reflections 💭

This week was mainly focused on tying up loose ends and completing outstanding work before the final submission. Although we were close to finishing, it was still a relief to hear that the submission deadline had been extended. We decided to move the video recording into the following week to allow ourselves more time to finish the dashboard properly.

The extension was particularly useful because some members of the team were unwell during the week or had last-minute work commitments. Having the extra time also gave us more flexibility and meant that we could focus on resolving merge conflicts, reviewing pull requests, and getting the final dashboard onto the `main` branch in a more controlled way.

One of the most useful parts of the week for me was being assigned as a reviewer on a couple of pull requests. Reviewing other people’s code helped me understand the structure of the application much better, particularly how the server logic, UI files, and `app.R` worked together. I found this more useful than only looking at isolated examples because I could see how the different components worked as part of the wider application.

This gave me more confidence to work on the Activity tab towards the end of the week. Earlier in the project, I was less comfortable with R Shiny and had mainly contributed through documentation, EDA, and project organisation. By this point, after reviewing other implementations and understanding the structure more clearly, I felt confident enough to build my own section of the dashboard. I found this a good indication of how much my understanding had developed over the course of the project.

Overall, this week felt less about making major design decisions and more about integration, review, and finishing the project to a good standard. For me, it also reinforced how useful code review can be for both quality assurance and learning from other members of the team.
