### Dashboard 1

They covered all aspects of the task really well, including their methodology for working as a group and the different working methods they used such as _Crazy 8s_. The dashboard itself looked very polished and the UI was particularly impressive, with clear links back to the intended stakeholder of a friendship group. One improvement could have been to include short explanations for less obvious metrics or how they were derived. For example, I was not familiar with the metric of $\text{VO}_{2}$ max and what it represented, so had to do a quick google. Overall, I really appreciated the presentation format and how clearly they covered each aspect of the project.

### Dashboard 2

The dashboard was very clear and the Fitbit branding was a nice touch that made the final product feel cohesive. They went into a lot of depth with their analysis and highlighted the main trends they had identified, which made the presentation feel like a final report being delivered back to the friendship group. I also liked how honest they were in their retrospective about some of the difficulties they experienced. This was relatable, as our group also had challenges towards the end of the project with balancing work commitments and sickness.

### Dashboard 3

I really liked the way they used the GitHub board to organise their work and attached meeting agendas following each of their catch-ups. This is definitely something I would like to take forward into our next group task, as it provided a clear record of progress and decisions. Keeping the requirements as a separate checklist was also a good way of dividing responsibility and ownership early on. The presentation itself was clearly well thought out and showed a strong approach to project organisation.

### Dashboard 4

The presentation and branding of this dashboard was really nice and it felt very well considered for the intended audience of a friendship group using the _"BitFit Awards"_. The visual identity helped make the dashboard feel more engaging and gave it a clear personality rather than feeling like a generic analytical tool. One idea from this project that I will definitely be carrying forward into everyday work is:

> [!TIP]  
> #### If we use AI, WE SHALL NOT use slop

A 10/10 mantra for setting expectations around the quality and use of AI-assisted work.

### Dashboard 5

The recording was definitely a little long, but anomalies and findings were highlighted really well throughout the presentation. I particularly liked that they linked some of their observations back to external factors such as a bank holiday, as this was something I had not considered in our own analysis. This reinforced the importance of thinking about the wider context behind patterns in the data, which is something that I aim to carry forward. The target stakeholder felt slightly less central to the dashboard design, and for a friendship group I think a little more time could perhaps have been spent on the UI and overall user experience.

### Dashboard 6

A lot more time was spent discussing the software architecture, which gave a useful insight into how the dashboard had been structured technically. I was interested in how they determined measures such as distance from the provided dataset. I assume that this would have been an estimate based on step count? It would also have been useful to understand how any forecasts included in plots were calculated. Including a little more explanation around these derived metrics would have made the analysis easier to interpret. The UI itself was really neat, cohesive, and consistent across the dashboard.
